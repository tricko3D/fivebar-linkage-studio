Gear ratio is motor revolutions per one output revolution (1:1 = direct, 3:1 = three motor turns per output turn). RPM is an assumed usable motor speed, not a torque-speed curve. The path animation checks both motor RPM and output torque under the simplified dynamics assumptions.

Motor torque must be the available running torque at your intended speed, not the motor's holding torque. A 1:1 ratio means direct drive. XY torque uses a conservative acceleration-direction estimate, an approximate fraction of link inertia and an assumed drag force, not a full dynamics model.

Structural model approximates the four links as two parallel idealized cantilever chains. It is not FEA and does not include pivot play, tool overhang, Z guide flex, buckling, print defects or motor speed curves.

The graphs evaluate a straight XY path with a triangular/trapezoidal acceleration profile and show estimated torque at the linkage output and RPM at the NEMA 17 shaft. They are comparative planning estimates, not measured motor characteristics or certified dynamic loads. They omit the full links’ rotational inertia, stepper rotor acceleration, motor torque-speed curves, and flex/backlash. A physical motor may stall even if a graph passes.

How to read the model

P is where the two distal links meet. G is the gripper tip. With a tool extension G moves beyond P along a selected distal link, so it changes the inverse kinematics, not just the drawing.
The mechanical estimates are simplified screening calculations, not FEA or a verified safe working load. Check pivot clearances, bearing stiffness, Z-axis overhang, print orientation, impacts and the motor torque-speed curves experimentally. The Z actuator is represented only by its mass.
